## Primary conclusion

**Primary conclusion statement:**

> Working memory imposes three structurally distinct constraints on learning — a fixed item-count ceiling (κ ≈ 3–4 chunks), a short temporal window (~2s phonological decay), and a processing-bandwidth bottleneck (central executive, finite attentional capacity). These constraints are architectural, non-trainable, and fixed across all learners. All effective learning strategies operate through one or more of exactly three mechanisms: (1) reducing extraneous or intrinsic load to keep L_total ≤ κ_WM (chunking, schema pre-activation, worked examples, split-attention reduction); (2) exploiting parallel channels to expand effective capacity without widening the bottleneck (dual coding, modality-separated instruction); or (3) progressively offloading sub-skills from WM to LTM via practice (automatization, spaced repetition). A fourth cross-cutting factor governs all three mechanisms: the learner's current schema state determines which strategies are beneficial and which are harmful (expertise reversal effect), making adaptive learner-state tracking a structural requirement, not an optional enhancement. The cross-domain convergence across Information Theory, Cache/Memoization, Acoustics/Resonance, and Fluid Dynamics independently confirms that the bottleneck is a design constraint, not a deficiency — and that the only engineering paths available are compression, parallel-channel exploitation, and load-offloading.

**Convergence-grounded support:**
- CS-1 (ceiling fixedness): N1+N2+N3+N5+N6 — 5 nodes
- CS-2 (schema/chunking): N1+N2+N3+N5+N6 — 5 nodes

**Qualifications from divergences:**

- LT-WM retrieval structures (Ericsson & Kintsch) may constitute a fourth, additive mechanism beyond the three enumerated above — this is not resolved and is flagged as an open probe (D-1).

## Generalization check

**Generalizes to:** The three-mechanism framework (compress, parallelize, offload) generalizes to any bounded-capacity sequential-processing system where:
- Capacity is architectural and fixed (non-trainable)
- Task content is structured with learnable regularities (domain regularity assumption — CS-6)
- A parallel processing channel is available (dual-coding applies to any dual-channel architecture)
- Skills can be decomposed into sub-skills amenable to isolated practice (automatization applies)

This includes: instructional design for any domain with learnable structure, adaptive tutoring systems, expert system training programs, and procedural skill acquisition in motor, perceptual, and cognitive domains.

**Scope boundary:** The framework generalizes within the acquisition phase (not expert-plateau or pure performance phases). It does not generalize to random/structureless domains (CS-6 violated), clinical populations below WM threshold, or creative open-ended tasks where WM search-space maintenance is desirable rather than minimized.

The generalization is not a one-off finding. The bottleneck + three-mechanism structure recurs across domains (confirmed by N2 cross-domain survey, 12 viable structural analogs). This is the structural signature of a **generalizable constraint**, not an artifact of a single experimental tradition.

   — Priority: HIGH (weakest link identified by N6 and V1 partial)

3. **[PROBE 3 — MEDIUM PRIORITY]** Does the "Resonant Cache" dual-function claim (schema pre-construction = both prefetch AND resonant amplification) generate empirically distinct predictions from standard advance-organizer research?
   — Why it matters: N2 blend claims two mechanistically distinct effects of schema pre-construction; standard advance-organizer research (Ausubel) typically measures only one (retention improvement). If the blend is correct, schema pre-construction should produce both lower WM load during encoding AND deeper retrieval depth on delayed tests — independently measurable as a two-dimensional outcome profile rather than a single benefit.
   — Test: Pre-learning schema activation condition with dual dependent measures: (1) secondary-task WM load during learning (prefetch prediction) and (2) cued-recall depth at 1-week delay (resonance prediction). If both are elevated vs. no-organizer control, the dual-function claim is supported.
   — Priority: MEDIUM (novel prediction from N2 blend; not yet tested in this specific form)

4. **[PROBE 4 — MEDIUM PRIORITY]** Does the expertise-reversal effect apply in schema-formable domains universally, or is there a domain-regularity threshold below which it does not emerge?

   — Test: Compare expertise-reversal onset across domains varying in regularity (chess > mathematics > creative writing > random lists). Measure point of worked-example inversion by novice-to-expert continuum. A regularity-threshold curve would bound the generalization of CA-2.
   — Priority: MEDIUM

5. **[PROBE 5 — LOW PRIORITY]** Does the high-span anxiety vulnerability paradox (CA-6) generalize beyond Beilock's procedural performance tasks to declarative learning tasks?
   — Why it matters: CA-6 is currently supported primarily by procedural choking-under-pressure data. If the paradox does not generalize to declarative domains, adaptive instructional design targeting high-span learners under anxiety requires different interventions by domain type.
   — Test: Replicate Beilock-style anxiety induction in a declarative knowledge-test condition with high-span and low-span learners. Measure performance decrement as a function of WM span.
