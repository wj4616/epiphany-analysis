## Primary conclusion

The creative-to-DSP precision gap is the architectural axis around which the entire system must be organized. It is simultaneously the primary engineering challenge (information degrades irreversibly across domain boundaries in a forward-only pipeline) and the primary commercial advantage (the wider the gap that the AI translation layer bridges, the more defensible the product). This dual identity — obstacle and moat — is the core structural insight confirmed independently across 6 analytical stages.

The system's viability depends on three conditions operating simultaneously: (1) coherent creative vision from the developer, (2) gap width within the RAG KB's bridgeable range (not too narrow for the moat to matter, not too wide for the RAG to bridge), and (3) time for RAG content to accumulate and calibration to progress. Removing any precondition causes the system to fail or degenerate (S6 extreme scenarios).

The forward-only pipeline is a structural property confirmed by all 3 observer frames in S4 — it creates irreversible information flow that cannot be corrected retroactively. This makes quality enforcement at each domain boundary critical: each boundary must act as a selective filter (S3 Illumination 3), and calibration thickness must adapt to boundary impedance and creative-intent complexity (S2 escape path). Confidence-score deltas across boundaries should serve as active diagnostics for resource allocation, not passive gap indicators (S2 emergent frame, S4 measurement frame, S5 flagged vagueness).

Four specific refinements to the current system design are supported by independent convergence:

1. **Adaptive calibration thickness**: Replace uniform calibration schedule with boundary-adaptive and intent-adaptive calibration. High-impedance boundaries (sound-design-to-DSP) always require thick matching; low-impedance boundaries (creative-to-sound-design) require thin matching. Complex creative intents require thicker matching than simple ones.

2. **Confidence scoring as impedance diagnostic**: Use confidence-score deltas across boundaries to actively route calibration resources, not just detect KB gaps.

3. **Standing-wave guard**: Add cross-boundary coherence checks to detect interacting calibration overcorrections before they create systematic bias.

4. **Out-of-distribution detection with paraphrase fallback**: Define a confidence floor below which the no-paraphrase rule yields to developer-confirmed paraphrase or explicit OoD flagging.
