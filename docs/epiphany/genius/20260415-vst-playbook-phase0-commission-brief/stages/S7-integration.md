## Primary conclusion

The Phase 0 commission brief for the VST plugin development playbook is not a planning document but an architectural specification — it encodes the system's binding constraint (creative-to-DSP translation with asymmetric verification) as its fundamental structure. The brief must specify three architectural subsystems:

**(1) Forward-only bridge chain with cache key integrity and point correction.** The bridge chain translates verbatim creative intent phrases into DSP parameter sets through six components, with point-correction error handling (fix at faulty node, re-run downstream only). Creative intent phrases serve as immutable cache keys — identity anchors, not just lookup mechanisms. The forward-only direction is an architectural invariant reflecting the irreversibility of creative processes, not merely a design choice. This subsystem is the system's core mechanism; removing it collapses the architecture.

**(2) Calibration schedule with shifting model/ear weight per development phase.** The developer is an improving sensor, not a fixed limitation. The brief should specify a weight schedule: early phases (0–2) trust the KB model at 80%, later phases (7–9) trust the developer's ear at 80%. This resolves the paradox of a C++ beginner making quality-#1 decisions: early development relies on structured knowledge, later development relies on calibrated perception. The calibration schedule makes the developer's improving competence part of the architecture.

**(3) Dual-vocabulary interface contract (perceptual stratum + structural stratum).** The brief must define two vocabulary strata: perceptual (creative/evaluation domains — DSP, sound design, UI, marketing language: "warm," "lush," "evolving") and structural (legal/commercial/architectural — licensing, KB architecture, technical stack, error correction protocols: "VST3 SDK 3.8.x," "Tier 1 gate," "point correction"). The bridge system handles perceptual-to-structural conversion. Each stratum uses its own vocabulary internally but references a shared interface (the verbatim creative intent phrase) at the boundary.

Two structural insights complete the conclusion:

**(4) Sound identity as architecture invariant.** The plugin's creative focus on psychedelic, ambient, pad, and psybient sounds determines software architecture: drone stability requires bounded memory allocation, ambient textures require smooth parameter evolution (slew limiting on every parameter), psychedelic layering requires independent signal chains per voice. The sound design vocabulary IS the architectural specification.

**(5) Scope exclusions as competitive moat, not limitation.** The exclusions of reverb, EQ, compression, and visual-UI-as-product plugins define the product by what it is NOT. In a marketplace where saturated segments consume development resources for marginal differentiation, exclusion IS the competitive strategy. The brief should frame exclusions as strategic definitions, not constraints.

The quality priority ordering (DSP > creative > marketing > licensing > UI > C++) defines minimum viable quality (MVQ) thresholds per phase — not feature requirements but quality floors below which the product ceases to be what it claims to be. Phase 4's MVQ is "audio quality passes DAW listening test with perceptual descriptors matching creative intent." Phase 7's MVQ is "UI is functional and reflects perceptual vocabulary." Phase 10's MVQ is "licensing protects the creative vocabulary."
