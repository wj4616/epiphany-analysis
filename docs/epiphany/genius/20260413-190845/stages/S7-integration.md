## Primary Conclusion

```xml

```

**Primary Conclusion**

The Pre-Mortem retrospective technique's documented effectiveness — Klein's 30% improvement in failure reason-identification — is most parsimoniously explained as the *removal of social suppression barriers* (voice suppression, hierarchy, optimism norms) that cause teams to under-report known risks under baseline conditions, rather than as a genuine cognitive prospective-hindsight effect that generates new information. The technique's primary structural mechanism is therefore a *permission structure for orphan knowledge* (IL2), not a cognitive enhancement of individual reasoning.

This conclusion is supported by four independent convergence signals (CS-1 through CS-4) arising from S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, and S6, and yields a set of structural predictions not available in the original guide:

1. **Effectiveness is inversely proportional to pre-existing psychological safety** — in high-blame cultures, the technique produces performative compliance; in flat teams, marginal gain approaches zero. Technique fidelity (framing quality, facilitation skill) is not the primary variance driver; pre-existing suppression intensity is.

2. **The Cassandra gap is the technique's most dangerous unaddressed failure mode** — detection quality can be high while prevention effectiveness is near zero, because the technique terminates its causal chain at ownership assignment without verifying execution capacity. A project can fail exactly as predicted in a Pre-Mortem whose outputs were never implemented.

3. **The voting mechanism is structurally biased and unfixable by facilitation** — it conflates probability × impact into a single social-salience dimension; the resulting prioritization systematically discards low-salience, high-probability systemic risks in favor of vivid, emotionally salient scenarios. This is a measurement architecture flaw, not a calibration error.

4. **Certainty framing has a dose-response ceiling** — below the ceiling, stronger framing produces more output; above it (excessive vividness, nocebo activation), failure contagion and expectation depletion reduce output quality and may lower team commitment. The guide's instruction to maximize vividness is unsupported and potentially counterproductive for vulnerable teams.

**Headline Insight (compressed):** The Pre-Mortem is not primarily a cognitive enhancement technique — it is an institutional permission structure that releases socially suppressed knowledge within a dose-optimal ritual frame. Its effectiveness is capped by pre-existing suppression intensity, its output is systematically biased by a structurally flawed voting mechanism, and its prevention value is largely disconnected from detection quality by the Cassandra gap.

Does the primary conclusion generalize beyond the Pre-Mortem technique?

**Generalizes to:** Any group deliberation technique whose stated benefit is "surfacing risks that would not otherwise emerge." The mechanism (social suppression removal, not cognitive enhancement) applies to: Red Team exercises, Futurespectives, FMEA facilitated sessions, sprint retrospectives, devil's advocate processes, and anonymous survey methods in organizational settings. ### V5 — Constructive Verification

**Constructive spec for the primary conclusion:**

The conclusion "Pre-Mortem gains are primarily explained by social suppression removal, not prospective-hindsight cognitive enhancement" is demonstrable by the following minimal procedure:

Administer two conditions to matched teams running identical pre-project risk identification tasks: (A) standard Pre-Mortem with certainty framing and facilitated session; (B) fully anonymous written risk survey with no temporal framing, no facilitator, no group interaction. Collect unique failure reasons per condition (deduplicated). Measure each team's pre-existing psychological safety using the Edmondson scale. If the gap between conditions A and B is proportional to pre-existing suppression intensity (higher suppression → larger A vs. B gap), and if the gap approaches zero in high-safety teams, the suppression-removal mechanism is confirmed and the cognitive-enhancement mechanism is not required.

**Minimality check:** Can any element be removed?
- "matched teams" — cannot remove; confound otherwise
- "identical pre-project tasks" — cannot remove; domain variation otherwise

- "unique failure reasons, deduplicated" — cannot remove; this is the Klein-style measurement that links to the 30% claim

No element is eliminable. Constructive spec is minimal.

## Open Questions and Next Probes

From divergence points D1–D2, V4 flags (none — all stages passed), and V6 exclusions:

**Probe 1 — What was Klein's control condition?**
*Why it matters:* The entire H-C1 hypothesis (suppression-removal as primary mechanism) hinges on whether Klein's baseline included social dynamics. If Klein's control was already fully anonymous, H-C1 is partially refuted and the cognitive mechanism must be retained. This single factual question resolves the V1 partial logic gap.
*Priority: HIGH* — highest discriminating power per unit effort; requires only a literature retrieval, not a new study.

**Probe 2 — Randomized comparison: Pre-Mortem vs. anonymous risk survey, stratified by Edmondson psychological safety score**
*Why it matters:* Directly tests H-C1 and H-C2. If Pre-Mortem gain over anonymous survey is moderated by pre-existing safety score (larger in low-safety teams, near-zero in high-safety teams), the suppression-removal mechanism is confirmed. This is the S6 primary falsification condition for H-C1.
*Priority: HIGH* — most decisive empirical test; requires approximately one research-lab Pre-Mortem study with matched controls.

**Probe 3 — Post-Pre-Mortem audit: implementation rate of ownership assignments at 30, 60, and 90 days**
*Why it matters:* The Cassandra gap (CS-2) predicts that a substantial fraction of Pre-Mortem-identified risks will not have implemented prevention strategies by project execution. No published measurement of this rate exists (identified in S1 success criterion #18 and S3 IL3 implication chain). A single organizational audit would provide a baseline. The Millikan assessment flags this as an asymmetric gap in the supporting evidence.
*Priority: HIGH* — closes the most dangerous epistemic gap (false mitigation confidence); requires organizational access but not a controlled experiment.

**Probe 4 — Vividness manipulation study to locate the dose-response ceiling (H-C5)**
*Why it matters:* The guide's instruction to maximize vividness is potentially counterproductive per S3 IL5 and S6 H-C5. Locating the dose-optimal vividness level would allow the guide's facilitation instructions to be refined. The discriminating condition is: three conditions (low / moderate / high vividness), matched teams, measured outputs and post-session morale. Expert vs. novice sub-group analysis tests H-C6.
*Priority: MEDIUM* — important but requires controlled experimental design with affect measurement; higher friction than Probe 1 or Probe 3.

**Probe 5 — Solo vs. team Pre-Mortem comparison on implementation rate (H-C7)**
*Why it matters:* The Cassandra gap hypothesis predicts that solo Pre-Mortems (decision-maker only, no handoff) will have dramatically higher implementation rates than team Pre-Mortems, despite lower identification breadth. If confirmed, the guide's assumption that team format is necessary is challenged, and a lightweight solo-Pre-Mortem variant becomes a high-value alternative for individual decision-makers.
*Priority: MEDIUM* — requires access to comparable solo and team decision-makers running Pre-Mortems on matched decisions; feasible in organizational research settings.

**Probe 6 — Tail-risk aggregation: what patterns exist in the discarded below-threshold items from Step 4?**
*Why it matters:* S4 (imbalance: permanent discard of sub-threshold items) and S5 (consequence Branch C, tail risk discarding) identify the discarded item set as a potential systemic-risk repository. No published analysis of this set exists. A qualitative study comparing discarded items against actual project failure causes would determine whether the voting threshold creates systematic blindspots.
*Priority: LOW* — valuable for technique refinement but lower discriminating power on the primary conclusion mechanism; requires post-project retrospective data collection.
